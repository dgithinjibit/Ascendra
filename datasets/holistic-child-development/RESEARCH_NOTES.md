# AI, Child Wellbeing, and Education Research Notes

## Common Sense Media / Common Sense Education

Common Sense Media’s Digital Literacy & Well-Being Curriculum describes a free, research-backed K–12 framework covering AI literacy, online safety, screen time, digital footprints, relationships, cyberbullying, information/media literacy, and healthy habits. The curriculum emphasizes practical classroom activities such as reflection, dilemma discussions, media analysis, and healthy technology choices. Source: https://www.commonsense.org/education/digital-literacy

The Common Sense Education AI Basics for K–12 Teachers course covers foundational AI literacy, how generative AI works, education-related safety considerations including data privacy, ethical issues, and reflective planning for appropriate classroom use. We use these as high-level design principles only; no course text or proprietary lesson content is copied. Source: https://www.commonsense.org/education/training/ai-basics-for-k-12-teachers

The Common Sense lesson What Is AI? defines learning objectives around distinguishing AI and generative AI and reflecting on benefits and drawbacks. It is listed for grades 6–12 and uses a short lesson format. The page states a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-NoDerivatives license for the lesson, so the dataset should cite the source and avoid remixing or reproducing the lesson. Source: https://www.commonsense.org/education/digital-citizenship/lesson/what-is-ai

## UNICEF

UNICEF’s Guidance on AI and Children, version 3.0, identifies 10 child-centred requirements: regulation and oversight; safety; privacy and data protection; fairness and non-discrimination; transparency, explainability and accountability; responsible rights-respecting practice; best interests, development and wellbeing; inclusion; skills and preparation; and an enabling environment. The guidance also highlights AI companions, harmful datasets, environmental impacts, AI-generated sexual abuse material, non-consensual intimate imagery, accessibility, and children’s participation. Source: https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children

## UNESCO

UNESCO’s Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research promotes a human-centred approach, data-privacy protection, age-appropriate use, ethical validation, pedagogical design, equity, meaningful use, and capacity-building for educators and learners. Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research

## OECD

The OECD’s 2025 report on children in the digital age frames child digital wellbeing as a four-pillar policy challenge: effective regulation and child-safe technology; digital literacy and skills with schools and teachers playing a central role; guidance for parents and caregivers; and cooperation that includes children’s perspectives. It also notes that online risk can reflect offline vulnerabilities and that policy should consider protection, empowerment, participation, and wellbeing together. Source: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/how-s-life-for-children-in-the-digital-age_0854b900-en/full-report/enhancing-child-well-being-in-the-digital-age-a-four-pillar-policy_42f060db.html

## Africa-focused context

CIPESA’s 2025 analysis argues that African child-centred AI design should include children’s voices from the beginning, address privacy and harmful content, extend digital literacy beyond basic internet safety to AI awareness, and use transparency, independent audit, data minimisation, and inclusive governance. These principles are relevant to Kenyan CBC implementation and global South contexts, but the article should be treated as contextual analysis rather than a dataset source for copied text. Source: https://cipesa.org/2025/07/elevating-childrens-voices-and-rights-in-ai-design-and-online-spaces-in-africa/

## Dataset design implications

The expanded dataset should use original synthetic scenarios mapped to these principles. Each record should include source principles, age and grade bands, learner agency, intended learning outcome, safe response pattern, adult-support route, localization notes, language considerations, accessibility considerations, privacy flags, and whether human review is required. The records must not diagnose children, infer sensitive traits, encourage secrecy, collect unnecessary personal data, or present AI as a replacement for educators, caregivers, counsellors, or safeguarding professionals.

## Licensing decision

The dataset’s original synthetic records and metadata should be released under **CC BY 4.0** to maximize reuse with attribution. Source descriptions and links remain provenance references; Common Sense lesson content is not copied into the dataset, and its NC-ND terms are not relicensed by Syncsenta. Third-party source materials remain under their original licenses. The dataset card should clearly separate Syncsenta-created content from external references.
